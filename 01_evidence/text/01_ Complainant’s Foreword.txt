Section 1: Complainant’s Foreword
On July 21st, 2024, after four years of being gaslit and seeing my life through someone
else's eyes, | made my first call for help. | said, "Police, please." | was met with a silence that
would last for a year, a silence that would push me to the very brink of my life.
This complaint documents what happens when a victim of crime Is not just ignored, but is
actively and systematically erased by the very institutions that are supposed to protect them.
The evidence within this bundle will prove, beyond any doubt, a coordinated institutional

failure by Sussex Police and its partner agencies. It will detail evidence tampering,
catastrophic safeguarding breaches, and a culture of complicity that chose to protect itself
rather than protect a human life.
But this is not just a story of my own survival. It is a story about my friend, Kim Box.
The same pattern of institutional failure, the same confusion, the same dismissal that |
experienced, is exactly how Kim died. She was failed by the same police force. When |
found myself on the same path, feeling the same despair and the same hopelessness, |
knew | had a choice. | could become another statistic, another victim whose family would be
left with nothing but questions.

Or | could fight.
| would never have ended my life. Not because | wasn't pushed to that edge—I was. But

because of Amy, and because of my little Milena, whose own mother is now looking after
Kim's 13-year-old daughter who found her mother dead. | could not let that happen again. |
could not let that snowball of grief and unanswered questions crush another family.
So | made a decision. | would not just survive; | would document my survival. | would walk
the path they laid for Kim, but | would record every step, every lie, every failure. | would
become the witness she never had.

What | have been through was harrowing. But this complaint is not just for me. It is for Kim. It
is for every victim whose last words were "police, please," and who was met with silence. It
is for their families, who deserve the closure of knowing that someone, finally, is holding this
broken system to account.
