As explained on its website, Brighton Fringe is an "open-access festival meaning
that anyone can pay our registration fee, find a venue and put on an event as
part of Brighton Fringe".

It continues: "Brighton Fringe sets out to stimulate, educate and entertain all
year round through a diverse range of events, performances and workshops.
"During May and part of June, Brighton Fringe welcomes local, national and

international performers and audiences as it showcases creativity in Brighton,
Hove and beyond.

"This vast celebration of the weird, wild and wonderful has grown out of and is
inspired by, home-grown talent. More than 30% of events are put on by artistic

companies based in Brighton & Hove."
Brighton Fringe says it is "committed to helping the arts flourish" and
is "completely open-access".

Brighton Fringe does not book performers, but is approached by people wishing

to put events on and be part of the event.

Who is taking part in Brighton Fringe and how to attend

There are hundreds of acts and events as part of the annual event this year.
This includes comedy acts, children's shows, theatrical performances,
workshops, tours, and more.

To view the full list or to book tickets to any of the events, visit the Brighton
Fringe website.

Tickets are available all day every day, and can be purchased online, at the
Brighton Fringe Box Office, or by calling 01273 91 72 72 between 10am to 5pm,
Monday to Friday.
All tickets for Brighton Fringe 2025 are e-tickets, which you can print out or show

on your phone.

Where is Brighton Fringe and how to get there
Brighton Fringe and its events are taking place in venues across the city.

Information on specific events and shows can again be found on the Brighton
Fringe website.

While Brighton Fringe officially began in 2006, fringe activity has been
happening alongside the Brighton Festival since 1967.

It is the largest annual arts festival in England and one of the largest fringe
festivals in the world, with more than 1,000 events in 2018.

Over the years, various famous faces have been a part of the Brighton Fringe,
such as Sir Peter Blake, who worked on the Beatles' album cover for Sgt.
Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band.
