3.39: The Charity Shield: How State-Funded Advocacy Was Weaponised for Narrative

Control
This analysis outlines a central and insidious tactic underpinning the systemic failures in my
case: the strategic co-option of charitable and advocacy organizations by Sussex Police to
create a public-facing illusion of accountability while actively suppressing my case. This
"Charity Shield" relies on the public's inherent trust in two concepts: that police investigate
crime and that charities act in good faith. By manipulating these perceptions, they created
a world where my reality was institutionally deniable.

The Strategic Purpose of Police-Funded Advocacy
The decision by Sussex Police to fund a "specialist" stalking charity like Veritas Justice must
be scrutinized not as an act of benevolence, but as a strategic investment in risk and
narrative management. In the wake of high-profile failures like the Shana Grice case, such a
partnership allows the police to appear to be taking stalking seriously. However, my

experience suggests its true function is one of containment:
1.

Containment and Control: Victims are directed to a police-funded advocate,
keeping them within a controlled ecosystem. This prevents them from seeking truly
independent legal counsel who might immediately identify police misconduct and

escalate it. The advocate becomes a buffer, managing the victim's expectations
downwards.
2.

Intelligence Gathering: The relationship provides the police with a back-channel to

monitor the victim's state of mind, their evidence, and their intentions, all under the
guise of "multi-agency support."
3.

Plausible Deniability: Tne existence of this "independent" charity allows the police

to claim they are following best practice and providing support, creating a PR shield
that makes it harder for the victim's claims of neglect to be believed.
The "Why Haven't You Reported It?" Trap

This institutional setup creates an impossible trap for the victim. When| tried to explain my
situation to others, | was met with predictable public logic: "Why haven't you reported it to the

police?" and "A charity can't just take your business." This is the intended outcome. The
system creates a world where the victim's reality is so at odds with public perception of how
institutions should work that the victim themselves appears paranoid or unreliable.

This forced me into a state of profound isolation, where my truth was systematically erased
because it did not fit the simplified narrative that crime = police procedures and charities =
good faith.
The Gaping Hole of Unaccountable Leadership
This entire structure serves to protect the "gaping hole" at the top of the command chain.
The untouchable senior decision-makers who allow these failures to persist can use both the
frontline officers and the co-opted charities as scapegoats. Failures are blamed on individual
“pad apples" or a "breakdown in communication" with the support service, while the systemic

strategy of containment remains unexamined. The charity shield ensures that the leadership
remains insulated from accountability.
Conclusion:
The funding and use of advocacy charities like Veritas Justice by Sussex Police is not a
solution to their systemic failings; it is a part of the problem. It is a sophisticated form of
institutional gaslighting that creates an illusion of Support while actively disempowering
victims. | allege that this model is a fundamental conflict of interest designed to manage
liability, not protect vulnerable people. The I|OPC must investigate this relationship not just as

a procedural issue, but as a potential conspiracy to obstruct justice,where the good name of
charity is used to provide cover for state-sanctioned neglect.
