3.3: The Leadership Vacuum & The Culture of Complicity
A Study in Strategic Abandonment & Scapegoating

Source: 3.317-04-25—-04.32_101_Call_“The Holiday Alibi"

This analysis asserts that the catalogue of failures documented in this complaint is not the
result of rogue actions by a few junior officers, but the direct consequence of a deliberate
and strategic leadership vacuum within Sussex Police. The evidence shows a systemic
culture of complicity that prioritises institutional self-preservation over public service, creating
a "bullshit machine" where accountability is impossible.
The Psychology of Complicity: How Good People Do Nothing The actions of the officers

involved cannot be understood as simple malice. They are, | allege, a textbook example of
how individuals can become complicit in harmful conduct through a process of moral

disengagement and institutional conformity. Individuals "view their actions as stemming from
the dictates of authorities rather than being personally responsible for them," allowing them
to be "spared self-condemning reactions." This is how a culture of complicity is born: not
from a desire to do harm, but from a desire to conform, to avoid trouble, and to remain part
of the group. The "little voices" of conscience are silenced by the fear of standing out.
The Scapegoat Protocol: A System Designed to Fail This culture of complicity is
maintained by a "Scapegoat Protocol." The evidence clearly shows that PC Jon Dixon was
intentionally positioned as the sole point of contact and failure for my case. This is a strategic
firewall. By channeling all complaints towards a single, junior officer, senior command
remains shielded from scrutiny. If the pressure becomes too great, the scapegoat can be

sacrificed, creating the illusion of accountability while the systemic rot remains untouched.
This is why, despite my repeated and documented complaints, my case was never truly

reassigned. The refusal to remove PC Dixon, and the subsequent assignment of a new
officer who reported to the same compromised supervisor (Sergeant Duffy), was not an
oversight; it was the system protecting itself. The real failure is not at the bottom of this
hierarchy; it is in the "gaping hole" of leadership at the top.
The "Holiday Alibi": Proof of Organised Abandonment The most stark proof of this
leadership vacuum is the "Holiday Alibi." Following my suicide disclosure on April 6, 2025,

the entire operational chain of command for my case—PC Jon Dixon, Sergeant Duffy, and
Inspector Palmer—was simultaneously unavailable for 17 days. This was not a coincidence;
it was an organized abandonment. In any high-risk case, a functioning command structure

would ensure delegated authority and continuous safeguarding oversight. The absence of
any such handover proves that no senior officer reviewed the case or assigned responsibility
during this critical period.

Conclusion & Demand for Investigation This was not a failure by PC Dixon; it was a

failure of the culture and command structure above him. Therefore, | demand the lOPC
investigation focus not only on the conduct of the named officers but, more importantly, on:

1.

Identifying the senior officer(s) with command responsibility for this case who were on

duty between April 6th and April 23rd, 2025, and failed to ensure safeguarding
continuity.
2.

Auditing the decision-making process that allowed the entire command chain for a
high-risk stalking victim to be on leave simultaneously without a formal, documented
handover to a competent authority.

3.

Investigating the systemic use of procedural loopholes (like GDPR and leave rotas)
as mechanisms for institutional avoidance and the scapegoating of junior officers.

The real failure is not at the bottom of this hierarchy. It is in the silence at the top.
